# Slide patterns

## Density budget — treat these as limits, not targets

| Element | Limit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Words per slide (title excluded) | **≤ 30** | above this students read instead of listening |
| Bullets per slide | **≤ 4** | more means you have two slides |
| Words per bullet | **≤ 8** | a full sentence belongs in the notes |
| Lines of body text | **≤ 6** | projection distance and back-row legibility |
| Font size (body) | **≥ 24 pt** | below this the back row cannot read it |
| Concepts per slide | **1** | two ideas on one slide means neither lands |

If a slide exceeds the budget, split it or move detail to the notes. Do not shrink the font and do
not compress the wording until it becomes cryptic.

## Choosing a layout by what the idea is

| The idea is… | Layout | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| one number that matters | a single large figure plus a one-line caption | the caption says what the number means, not what it is |
| a comparison | two columns with the same three dimensions on both sides | never compare along different dimensions |
| a sequence or process | 3–5 steps, left to right or top to bottom, with arrows only where order is real | more than five steps is two slides |
| a location or structure | a diagram or map with the labels already placed | an unlabelled diagram is a puzzle, not a slide |
| a cause and effect | the cause on the left, the effect on the right, an explicit arrow | state the mechanism in the notes |
| something that changes over time | two states side by side, or one chart with the change marked | do not animate a comparison the students need to see at once |
| a question you want asked | the question alone, large, on an otherwise empty slide | this is the most under-used layout |
| a photograph or artwork | full-bleed image, minimalist caption | say what the students should notice |

## Deck-level shape

- **Opening slide**: the topic and the one question the lesson answers. Not a table of contents —
  a contents slide tells students what is coming instead of making them want it.
- **Every 4–6 slides**: something that changes the mode — a question slide, a short task, a check.
- **Closing slide**: the answer to the opening question, plus what the students should be able to do
  now. Not 「谢谢观看」.
- **Slide count**: roughly one per 1.5–2 minutes of lesson. A 45-minute lesson is about 15–22 slides,
  and fewer if much of it is discussion or activity.

## Self-check before handing over

1. Every slide is within the density budget, or was deliberately split.
2. The spine reads as a coherent sequence with the screen off.
3. The opening slide states the question; the closing slide answers it.
4. No slide carries two concepts.
5. Every diagram is labelled; every chart has its axes named and its unit stated.
6. The detail that used to be on the slides is in the teacher's notes.
7. Slide count matches the duration.
8. No framework names, no decorative imagery doing no work.
